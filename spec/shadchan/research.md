# Shadchan Mode — survey research

What shadchanim told us in the ZugDesk survey, October 2026.
This file is the anonymized analysis only. The raw answers are **not** in the repo (it is public):
no names, no submission IDs, no timestamps, no per-person rows. Quotes are word for word except
where a detail could point to a real person; those spots are marked `[…]`.

## 1. What we have

- **26 responses** (the spreadsheet has 27 rows, one of which is the header). All from the revised (v2) survey.
- Survey language: 24 English, 1 Hebrew, 1 Russian. One English-form answer was written in Russian, so at least 2 Russian speakers.
- 12 of 26 wrote something in the free-text question. Most are one sentence.
- Everyone who answered is active: nobody chose "None" for workload, nobody chose "Neither" for device.

### Limits — read before trusting any number

- **Small and self-selected.** 26 people who chose to answer a link, mostly English speakers. Treat counts as direction, not proof. A difference of 1–2 answers means nothing.
- **The survey asked what is difficult, not what they want.** Nobody "asked for a feature". The ranking below is built from the difficulties they ticked plus what they wrote.
- **"Choose up to two"** on difficulties and barriers keeps every count low. A task with 3 ticks is not unimportant; it just was not in someone's top two.
- **The intro said the app is to "organize" work**, which may have steered answers towards organizing.
- The old columns "Most useful feature" and "Preferred device" are empty: nobody answered the first version of the survey.

| 2 | Tracking suggestions, answers and progress | 7 (27%) | 7 | Who was suggested to whom, what each side said, where it stands |
| 2 | Remembering whom to contact or follow up with | 7 (27%) | 8 | Who is waiting for me, whom am I waiting for |
| 4 | Finding information about people in my records | 4 (15%) | 5 | Someone calls; find that person's details fast |
| 5 | Avoiding repeated or duplicate suggestions | 3 (12%) | 3 | "Did I already suggest these two?" |

**Grouped into three jobs** (how many ticked at least one task in the group):

- **Keeping the pipeline moving** (tracking + follow-up): **11 of 26**.
- **Finding the right person** (suitable people + finding info in records): **11 of 26**.
- **Keeping records in order** (saving/updating + duplicates + finding info): **10 of 26**.

The three jobs are about equal. No single feature wins; the pain is that the work is spread over three places and nothing ties it together.

### By workload (small groups — direction only)

- **1–5 singles (6):** spread thin; 2 of 6 say nothing is hard.
- **6–20 singles (8):** tracking (3), follow-up (3), finding people (3). The classic "I need a pipeline" group.
- **21–50 singles (5):** finding info (2), finding people (2), follow-up (2), saving/updating (2). Records start to hurt here.
- **More than 50 (4):** no pattern at all: one found a system that works, one is "behind", one says people are too picky, one ticked tracking.

## 4. In their words

**Finding the right person**
- "Reading through many profiles to find a proper suggestion for a shidduch client."
- "Takes a lot of time of looking multiple groups where data not sorted at all"
- "Trying to find a shidduch for […a hard-to-match single with a specific requirement…]."

**Finding someone's information fast**
- "It happens often that someone calls about a girl or boy and it takes me embarrassingly long to find their information"

**Volume and time**
- "It's more that the sheer volume of work is daunting. Not really 'overlooked' situations, but just 'behind'"
- "Facilitating Shidduchim is time consuming"

**Losing information**
- "If information is gone because I forgot to add a person to my Google drive folder. Or accidentally deleted a wrong folder"

**Steps done in the wrong order**
- "A lot of effort (about a month) went into setting up a boy and a girl, families meeting etc. Even though we did Dor Yesharim, the results were not compatible. They never went out. All the effort and back and forth could have been avoided had Dor Yesharim been done at an earlier point."
- (translated from Russian) "There are plenty of situations where people spend a long time on checks, and then one meeting is enough to refuse to continue the shidduch."

**Waiting for the other side**
- "[…] Usually he sees an auto suggestion and asks me to find out more about her or ask her certain question; often the other side does not answer or takes too long to answer. On the other hand I myself have little belief in most of the suggestions he asks for"

**"My way already works"**
- "BH, At this point after many years of being a Shadchan and dating coach I found the system that works for me, so not much is overlooked on my end."
- "It happens, but I try to write it down."

**Outside any app's reach**
- "People are too picky"

**Barrier, in their own words**
- "Cost?"

## 5. What would stop them from trying a new tool

(choose up to two)

| Barrier | Answers |
|---|---|
| Nothing in particular | 8 (31%) |
| Time needed to learn how to use it | 7 (27%) |
| Privacy or confidentiality concerns | 7 (27%) |
| Having to enter the same information twice | 4 (15%) |
| Having to move my existing records | 4 (15%) |
| I prefer my current methods | 3 (12%) |
| It might not work on my device or internet filter | 1 |
| Something else: cost | 1 |

- "Enter twice" and "move my records" are the same fear, **typing work**: 7 different people ticked at least one. So it is a three-way tie at 7: **learning time, privacy, typing work**.
- Privacy worry sits in the middle groups: 5 of the 7 work with 6–50 singles. None of the four heaviest shadchanim (50 or more) ticked it.
- All 7 who worry about privacy already keep this information in WhatsApp. So the worry is not "it must never be digital"; it is "why should I trust a new, unknown tool with it".
- The heaviest group (more than 50) has the fewest objections: 3 of 4 said "nothing in particular".

## 6. Must-have and nice-to-have

This section is interpretation (Claude's reading of the numbers and quotes), not something respondents said in these words.

### Must-have

| # | What | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Find any person in seconds** by name, phone or any word in their profile | 4 ticked + the "embarrassingly long" quote. It is the moment the shadchan is on the phone with someone. |
| M2 | **One place per suggestion**: who with whom, each side's answer, where it stands, what was said | 7 ticked tracking. Today this lives in WhatsApp threads and memory. |
| M3 | **A follow-up list**: who is waiting for me, whom am I waiting for, since when | 7 ticked + the "other side does not answer" quote. |
| M4 | **Search my own singles to build a shortlist** (age, community, and so on). The shadchan decides; the app only narrows. | 9 ticked, the top single item. |
| M5 | **Almost no typing to get a profile in**: paste or forward from WhatsApp/email, keep the PDF/Word/photo as it is | 81% live in WhatsApp, 62% in email; 7 fear typing work. |
| M6 | **Same data on phone and computer** | 15 of 25 use both equally; only 5 are phone-only. |
| M7 | **Privacy that is easy to explain** in one sentence: where the data is and who can see it | 7 named it as a reason not to try. |
| M8 | **Useful in the first five minutes**, nothing to study | 7 named learning time. |
| M9 | **Hard to lose data**: backup, and undo for deletes | One strong quote only, but for a shadchan losing the records is the worst possible failure. |

### Nice-to-have

| # | What | Why it is not a must |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | "You already suggested these two" warning | Only 3 ticked it, but it comes almost free once M2 exists. Cheap; do it early. |
| N2 | A checklist per shidduch that puts the early checks first | Two quotes, not asked in the survey. Promising; needs a few conversations first. |
| N3 | Bringing in old records (Excel, Drive folders, another app) | 4 ticked "moving my records", but most have no records to move: they have WhatsApp and paper. |
| N4 | Sorting profiles that arrive in WhatsApp groups | One quote. Related to M5. |
| N5 | Automatic match suggestions | Nobody asked for the app to choose. Two respondents doubt suggestions in general. M4 comes first. |
| N6 | Hebrew and Russian | 2–3 of 26. |
| N7 | Working behind internet filters | 1 of 26, but for that person it is a full stop. Keep the app a plain, simple website. |
| N8 | Working together with other shadchanim or families | **0 of 26** chose coordination as a difficulty. |

### What no app will fix

"People are too picky", the other side not answering, the sheer amount of work, cases that are simply hard to match. The app can make these visible (who has been waiting, for how long); it cannot solve them. It should not promise to.

## 7. Surprises

1. **Almost nobody uses a spreadsheet** (2 of 26). The real "system" is WhatsApp + email + paper + memory. The competitor is not Excel; it is the chat history.
2. **Phone and computer equally is the norm** (15 of 25). Single Mode was designed phone-first and keeps data on one device. That does not fit most shadchanim.
3. **The top difficulty is matching, not organizing.** And part of it (not enough suitable people, pickiness) is not a software problem.
4. **The things an "organizer" most obviously does rank last**: saving profiles (3) and avoiding duplicates (3).
5. **Zero people chose coordination** with families or other shadchanim.
6. **The busiest shadchanim object the least.** Three of four with more than 50 singles said nothing would stop them. Privacy worry is in the middle group.
7. **Internet filter: only 1.** Expected more in this community. (It may be under-counted: people behind strict filters are less likely to reach an online survey.)
8. **Process pain was volunteered twice** without being asked: checks done too late, or too long, before a first meeting.
9. **A quarter already use a shidduch app or website** (6 of 26), and they still tick tracking and follow-up as hard. Existing sites help find people; they do not run the shadchan's own pipeline.
10. **A single's "auto suggestion" from a site becomes the shadchan's work**: the shadchan is asked to check it out. The shadchan is partly a go-between for other platforms.
11. **4 of 26 say nothing is hard and 8 of 26 say nothing would stop them.** A third are easy to get to try; a sixth do not need it.
12. **Free text was thin** (12 answers, mostly one line). The survey tells us *which* jobs hurt, not *how* they are done today.

## 8. Where shadchanim disagree

| Topic | One side | Other side |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a problem at all? | "I found the system that works for me"; 4 say nothing is hard; 3 prefer their current methods | "The sheer volume of work is daunting… just 'behind'" |
| Privacy | 7 would hesitate because of it | 8 say nothing would stop them, including the busiest |
| Device | 5 mainly phone | 5 mainly computer (15 both) |
| Is matching a tool problem? | 9 find it hard to find suitable people; "data not sorted at all" | "People are too picky"; "I myself have little belief in most of the suggestions" |
| Old records | 4 fear having to move them | Most have nothing structured to move |
| Existing shidduch apps | 3 of the 6 users would try something new without conditions | 2 of the 6 prefer what they have |
| Among the busiest (more than 50) | One has it solved | One is behind, one blames pickiness, one struggles with tracking |

## 9. Still unknown — worth five short conversations

The survey cannot answer these, and the design depends on them:

1. Walk me through the last suggestion you made, from the first idea to the last message. Where did you write things down?
2. When someone sends you a profile, what exactly do you do with it in the next minute?
3. You use phone and computer: what do you do on which? (This decides how much "same data on both" matters on day one.)
4. What does "private" mean to you: who must never see this, and what would make you trust a new tool?
5. How do you decide the order of checks before a first meeting?
