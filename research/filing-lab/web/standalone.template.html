      Benchmark

      Every engine gets the same made-up notes. Two questions are scored separately: did the engine understand the note? and did the note end up in the right place? The filing step was also run on the correct understanding of every note (130 of 130 filed right), so a mistake here belongs to the engine.

      A and B take roughly 2–10 seconds per note on a normal PC, so the holdout set takes a few minutes per model.

      What I found

      Typing a note and having it filed in the right place is mostly an organising problem, not an AI problem. Once a note is understood correctly, ordinary app logic files it right: 130 of 130 test notes, including “which Mrs. Cohen?”, “the Sarah you met last year”, “Leah’s reference”, and never touching your own answer.

      The three engines in this file

      • A · a tiny AI model (Qwen3 0.6B by default) and B · a small AI model (Qwen3 1.7B), both running on this computer’s graphics card, with the same instructions and the same forced output format. Comparing them answers “is tiny enough, or is a bit bigger clearly better?”. Other sizes are in the model menus.
      • C · no AI: word lists, a few sentence rules and your saved contacts. Instant, nothing to download, works on any phone.
      • GLiNER2.5 (a 74M-parameter extraction model, my first-choice tiny AI) has no browser version yet, so it is only in the PC kit.

      The best design without AI

      1. Write where it happened. On Leah’s page, or in the “Call ended: what happened?” card. The destination is then known. The same note “Reference didn’t answer, call tomorrow” needs a question from Home, but is one tap on Leah’s page (try it with Typing from).
      2. Names become chips as you type, like @-mentions. Two Mrs. Cohens appear as two chips with city and last call, so it is settled while typing.
      3. Dates are recognised as you type (“in two days” → Wed). Vague words ask for a day instead of inventing one.
      4. Buttons from the record’s state. Leah, waiting for her answer: She said yes · She said no · Still thinking. Mrs. Cohen, looking for me: Has an idea · Nothing yet · Will call me.
      5. Open loops instead of categories. Nearly every note updates something already open (waiting for Leah, Mrs. Cohen working on it, references to call). Filing becomes “which of this person’s one to three open items does this advance?”.

      Recommendation

      Build ZivugMatch’s filing on the no-AI design above, with the shared filing step deciding what is saved with one tap and what needs your OK. Add an AI engine later only as an optional “smart reading” helper, and only if, on the holdout set, it beats Engine C’s 89% by about 5 points or more, invents no more than C, and is small and fast enough for your phone. On the phone, only a tiny model such as GLiNER2.5 Small (about 0.1 GB) is realistic; a 1.7B model is 1 GB and takes seconds per note.

      Getting better over time without collecting conversations

      • Your corrections (“Rivky” is Rivka Adler, “shayach” means yes) become personal word-list entries, stored only on your device.
      • A tiny model can be trained on thousands of made-up notes, then shipped as a small update that must pass this benchmark first.
      • Optional sharing, if ever: only the pattern of a phrase with names removed, which you read before sending.

      Requirements for this file

      Chrome or Edge on Windows (WebGPU), internet for the first use of each AI model, and about 2 GB of free graphics memory for the 1.7B model. Engine C, the examples and the saved results work in any browser, offline.
